The handoff is where interfaces go to die

Design produces a picture. Engineering produces a thing. In between sits every decision that actually determines whether the interface is any good, and most teams leave that gap unassigned.

What happens at 360 pixels. What the empty state says. Where focus goes when the dialog closes. Whether the chart is still readable when the series count doubles. Whether the animation has a path for someone who gets motion sick. None of it is in the mock, and none of it is in the ticket.

So it gets decided by whoever notices last — usually under deadline, usually badly, usually by someone who inherited the file.

I've spent twelve years working both sides of that line specifically so there is no line. In practice that means prototyping in the browser on day one rather than arguing about a static comp for three weeks, and treating accessibility and performance as part of the same pass as the layout rather than a cleanup sprint that never gets scheduled.

It is not a glamorous pitch. The best compliment this kind of work gets is that nobody mentions it: nothing jumped, nothing trapped focus, nothing took four seconds, and the thing you wanted was where you expected it.
