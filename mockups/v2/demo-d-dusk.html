I'm a front-end engineer who came up through design, which means I've spent twelve years on the side of the handoff where the interesting decisions actually get made — what happens at 360px, what the empty state says, where focus lands when the dialog closes. Those never appear in a mock. They get decided by whoever notices last.

The best compliment my work gets is that nobody mentions it. Nothing jumped, nothing trapped focus, nothing took four seconds.

Most recently that has meant data visualisation — multi-series billing charts that had to stay interactive and legible on a phone without forking into a second component — and motion that teaches, like a scroll-sequenced explainer for an audience of security engineers who would have spotted a wrong metaphor instantly.

The rest is the part that doesn't demo well and matters most: component systems other engineers can extend without asking me first, accessibility built in the same pass rather than retrofitted, and performance budgets that someone actually measures.
