Search and discovery

SEO and AI search: what a lean B2B team should prioritize

By Nathan MooreOctober 5, 20264 min read

A lean team needs a search plan that produces useful pages and verifiable improvements. Start with the questions your buyers need answered, make those answers accessible, and measure what the available reports actually show. Treat promises of guaranteed AI mentions or a special ranking formula as claims that require evidence.

IN THIS ARTICLE

  2. Choose a buyer question before choosing a keyword

Use current platform guidance to set boundaries

Google's July 2026 guide to generative AI search says its AI features build on core Search systems. It does not require special schema or an llms.txt file. Meeting its requirements also does not guarantee indexing or visibility.

For technical eligibility, the guide says pages must be indexed and eligible for a search snippet, and the site must be included in Search generative AI features through Search Console. Check the current settings and documentation for your own property.

That guidance describes Google. Do not assume every answer service uses identical controls or reports. When someone recommends a tactic, ask which platform it affects, what documentation supports it, and how a useful result would be observed.

Choose a buyer question before choosing a keyword

Build a small question inventory from sales conversations, support themes, product documentation, and the language prospects use. Keep the evidence beside each question. A repeated sales objection and a speculative keyword idea should not look equally validated in the backlog.

For each question, decide what an adequate answer requires. A feature definition might need a concise product page. A migration question could need a sequence, prerequisites, and limitations. A comparison needs explicit evaluation criteria and current sources.

Hypothetical example: a buyer asks whether an approval tool replaces their accounting system. The useful page explains the boundary between the systems, shows the handoff, names supported connections, and describes what remains manual. Repeating the phrase accounting automation in several headings would not answer that question.

Make a few pages complete enough to use

Choose the questions closest to a real buying decision and assign an owner with access to the product facts. Provide a direct answer, the relevant evidence, an example, and the limitations. Link to the next resource a reader needs, even when that resource is documentation rather than a sales form.

Use original material you can support: a workflow explanation, a tested implementation note, a clearly labeled calculation, or a permissioned project account. Do not dress up a hypothetical example as client experience.

Google's helpful-content guidance asks whether a page adds original value and satisfies its intended audience. It also rejects the idea of a preferred word count. Use completeness of the answer as your editorial stopping point.

Fix technical obstacles with a specific diagnosis

Ask the implementation owner to check the pages you want discovered. Confirm that they load at the intended address, contain the expected content, are linked from relevant parts of the site, and have deliberate indexing and canonical settings.

When something is missing from search, record the affected URL and the evidence before prescribing a fix. A reporting gap, an access restriction, duplicate addresses, and weak page content are different problems. A general score rarely tells you which change is justified.

Preserve useful addresses during a redesign when possible. If URLs must change, create an explicit mapping. Google's site-move guide describes mapping old addresses to new ones, configuring redirects, and monitoring the move. Treat this as launch work with an owner.

Keep publication and revision dates honest

Use the date an article was actually first published. When you make a substantive revision, identify the update accurately. Do not create an invented history to make a new library appear established, or change dates merely to make unchanged advice appear current.

Google's helpful-content guidance specifically cautions against changing dates without meaningful changes. More fundamentally, a buyer should be able to understand whether a claim reflects today's product, an earlier version, or an illustrative scenario.

Keep a lightweight review record for material that changes: integration support, product comparisons, technical instructions, and platform guidance. Schedule review around known changes rather than rewriting useful pages without a reason.

Report visibility separately from business outcomes

As documented in October 2026, Search Console's Generative AI performance report includes impressions from AI Overviews and AI Mode, with page, country, date, and device views. Google says low impression volume can prevent the report from appearing.

An impression is evidence of visibility under that report's definition. It does not establish that someone visited, became a qualified lead, or bought because of the page. Keep those observations separate in your monthly review.

Choose the next action from the evidence: repair an access problem, strengthen an incomplete explanation, update a factual claim, or investigate a buyer question. Use the low-traffic measurement framework when the available numbers cannot support a reliable performance conclusion.

THE PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

Publish answers worth finding, remove specific technical obstacles, preserve truthful provenance, and distinguish search visibility from commercial impact.
