What you'd be hiring

Someone who finishes the unglamorous half

Most front-end work is pleasant until the edges arrive: the long string, the empty state, the third concurrent request, the person navigating with a keyboard. Those edges are where interfaces earn their reputation, and they are most of what I do.

I came up through design, so I'm comfortable owning the look when there isn't one yet — and I've spent enough years in implementation to know which beautiful ideas will quietly cost a team a year. The useful skill is telling those apart early.

  • Component systems other engineers extend without asking me first — which is the only real test of whether a system works.
  • Data visualisation that stays interactive and legible at 360px, not just a desktop chart with a smaller font.
  • Motion that means something, with a reduced-motion path that teaches the same thing without moving.
  • Accessibility in the same pass as the layout. Retrofitting focus order costs several times what building it does.
  • Performance budgets someone actually measures, and layout that doesn't move after it paints.
